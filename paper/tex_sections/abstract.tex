\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
	
Empirical regression coefficients can depend on small subsets of the estimation sample. Subset deletion can diagnose such sensitivity, but the behaviour and interpretation of Most Influential Sets (MIS) across common empirical settings remain insufficiently characterised. This thesis investigates fixed-\(k\) MIS as a diagnostic of target-coefficient sensitivity. Three simulation studies examine contamination, robust estimation, and model misspecification, while empirical audits apply a full-refit protocol to ten studies. The simulations show mechanism-dependent alignment between unusualness and coefficient influence, and distinguish influence localisation from estimator stability. Misspecification can also generate concentrated sensitivity without planted contamination, although linear endogeneity provides a fitted-span invariance limit in the simulated design. In the empirical audits, eight of ten coefficients cross zero along at least one searched deletion path within an approximately 5\% budget. Overall, MIS characterises where finite-sample coefficient sensitivity is concentrated and how its structure varies across mechanisms and applications.
