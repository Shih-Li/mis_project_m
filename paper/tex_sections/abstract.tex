\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
	
Empirical regression coefficients can depend on small subsets of the estimation sample. This thesis studies concentrated target-coefficient sensitivity using fixed-\(k\) Most Influential Sets (MIS), which identify the size-\(k\) deletion set that maximises movement in a prespecified coefficient. Three simulation studies examine contamination, robust estimation, and model misspecification, while empirical audits apply a full-refit protocol to ten studies. The simulations show that unusualness and coefficient influence are mechanism specific, and that influence localisation is distinct from estimator stability. Misspecification can also generate concentrated sensitivity without planted contamination, although linear endogeneity provides a fitted-span invariance limit in the simulated design. In the empirical audits, eight of ten coefficients cross zero along at least one searched deletion path within an approximately 5\% budget. Overall, MIS characterises where finite-sample coefficient sensitivity is concentrated and how its structure varies across mechanisms and applications.

\textbf{Keywords:}
