\section{Empirical Application Details}
\label{app:application-details}

This appendix documents the ten empirical audits summarized in \autoref{sec:application}. Each application fixes one target estimand, validates its full-sample coefficient, constructs an MIS-compatible linear representation, and searches deletion sets in both directions of coefficient movement. The default deletion budget is
\[
k=1,\ldots,\lfloor 0.05N\rfloor,
\]
subject to study-specific feasibility restrictions. Every selected deletion set is subsequently evaluated using the validated estimator associated with the frozen audit specification. The reported paths therefore record feasible leave-set-out refits of the audited coefficient.

For comparability across applications, define
\[
R_k
=
\frac{\widehat{\beta}_{-S_k}}
{\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}},
\]
where \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}\) is the validated full-sample coefficient and \(\widehat{\beta}_{-S_k}\) is the full-refit coefficient after deleting the selected size-\(k\) set. A \(50\%\) attenuation is reached when \(R_k\leq0.5\), a \(50\%\) amplification when \(R_k\geq1.5\), and a zero crossing when \(R_k\leq0\), with the direction normalised relative to the sign of the full-sample coefficient.

\autoref{fig:application-all-paths} displays the complete audit paths for all ten applications using a common normalisation. Each panel reports \(R_k\) against the deleted share of the estimation sample, with common reference lines for the full-sample coefficient and zero crossing.

\subsection{Study 01: Mobile Money}
\label{app:application-01}

The mobile-money audit targets the Mobile Disburse coefficient, \texttt{treatment3}, in the fixed-effects OLS specification of \textcite{riley2024resisting}. The validated sample contains \(N=2{,}639\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=69.2072\). The MIS representation replaces the absorbed randomization-strata fixed effects with explicit indicators, while full-refit validation uses the original fixed-effects estimator. The first searched zero crossing occurs after deleting \(41\) observations, approximately \(1.55\%\) of the estimation sample.

\subsection{Study 02: Vaccine Delivery}
\label{app:application-02}

The vaccine-delivery audit targets the door-to-door treatment coefficient \texttt{treat\_dtd} in Table 1, column (1), of \textcite{meriggi2024last}. The validated fixed-effects sample contains \(N=12{,}096\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=0.2935\); the alternative treatment arm remains in the regression as a nuisance regressor. The search uses explicit fixed-effect indicators and full-refit validation preserves the paper's fixed-effects estimator. The coefficient does not cross zero within the approximately \(5\%\) audit budget, although a \(50\%\) attenuation is first reached after \(505\) deletions, approximately \(4.17\%\) of the sample.

\subsection{Study 03: Self-Esteem}
\label{app:application-03}

The self-esteem audit uses the first-follow-up full-sample specification from Table 5 of \textcite{gallego2024s} and targets the treatment coefficient for standardized Rosenberg self-esteem. The validated sample contains \(N=634\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=0.2317\). Heteroskedasticity-robust covariance estimation does not alter the OLS point estimate, so the same linear specification is used for the search and full-refit validation. The coefficient does not cross zero within the audit budget; a \(50\%\) attenuation is first reached after \(16\) deletions, approximately \(2.52\%\) of the sample.

\subsection{Study 04: Health Information and Survival}
\label{app:application-04}

The health-information audit targets \texttt{learnhivpos} in the reported OLS companion specification for survival in \textcite{ciancio2025surviving}. The validated sample contains \(N=2{,}332\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=-0.1566\). The first searched zero crossing occurs after deleting \(17\) observations, approximately \(0.73\%\) of the estimation sample.

The source study's preferred causal specifications use instrumental variables and efficient linear GMM. Those estimators lie outside the current linear MIS search framework. The audit therefore characterises sensitivity of the reported OLS companion coefficient and does not constitute a deletion audit of the preferred IV/GMM estimate.

\subsection{Study 05: Cash versus In-Kind Transfers}
\label{app:application-05}

The cash-versus-in-kind audit considers the aggregate food-consumption analysis of \textcite{cunha2014testing}. The target is the contrast between the in-kind and equal-value cash treatment effects, represented directly by \texttt{ik\_minus\_eq\_cash}. The validated no-village-controls specification contains \(N=10{,}985\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=-0.8861\). The algebraic reparameterization leaves the model span, fitted values, residuals, and estimation sample unchanged. The first searched zero crossing occurs at \(k=1\), approximately \(0.009\%\) of the estimation sample.

The fully controlled aggregate-consumption replication retains unresolved differences from the reported benchmark. The audit therefore uses the validated no-village-controls specification, and its sensitivity results apply to that contrast.

\subsection{Study 06: Workforce Grant}
\label{app:application-06}

The workforce-grant audit targets \texttt{treat\_HD} in the intent-to-treat employment specification of \textcite{mcintosh2022using}. The validated fixed-weight sample contains \(N=1{,}770\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=0.0231\). The search represents weighted least squares through the exact square-root-weight transformation \(y_i^*=\sqrt{w_i}y_i\) and \(X_i^*=\sqrt{w_i}X_i\), while full-refit validation uses the original weighted estimator. The first searched zero crossing occurs after deleting \(10\) observations, approximately \(0.56\%\) of the estimation sample.

\subsection{Study 07: Sanitation Spillovers}
\label{app:application-07}

The sanitation-spillover audit targets \texttt{neighbor\_high} in the treated-household specification of \textcite{deutschmann2024spillovers}. The validated sample contains \(N=3{,}757\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=-0.00976\). The source specification uses post-double-selection LASSO for nuisance-control selection; the audit reproduces this step on the full sample and freezes the selected controls throughout the deletion path. Re-running selection after each deletion would define a different sensitivity problem. The first searched zero crossing occurs after \(33\) deletions, approximately \(0.88\%\) of the estimation sample.

\subsection{Study 08: Access to Finance}
\label{app:application-08}

The access-to-finance audit targets the competitor-treatment exposure coefficient \texttt{inter4post} for firm log sales in Table 3 of \textcite{cai2024indirect}. The validated sample contains \(N=8{,}612\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=-0.08555\). The MIS representation uses explicit firm fixed effects, while full-refit validation uses the paper-specific fixed-effects estimator. The first searched zero crossing occurs after deleting \(20\) observations, approximately \(0.23\%\) of the estimation sample.

\subsection{Study 09: Immunization Nudges}
\label{app:application-09}

The immunization audit targets the pooled immunizations-per-dollar policy coefficient in \textcite{banerjee2025selecting}. The validated village-month sample contains \(N=7{,}370\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=0.004016\). The audit reconstructs variable selection and policy pooling on the full sample and freezes the resulting specification throughout the deletion path. Fixed village-population weights are represented through the same square-root-weight transformation used in Study 06, and full-refit validation uses the validated weighted model. The first searched zero crossing occurs after deleting \(214\) observations, approximately \(2.90\%\) of the estimation sample; the geographic composition of this set is examined in \autoref{fig:application-spatial-concentration}.

\subsection{Study 10: Electricity Information}
\label{app:application-10}

The electricity-information audit targets \texttt{T1} in the Table A5 linear-probability robustness specification of \textcite{westberg2025power}. The validated sample contains \(N=1{,}819\) observations, with \(\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}=-0.1168\). The first searched zero crossing occurs after deleting \(63\) observations, approximately \(3.46\%\) of the estimation sample.

The source study's headline specification uses ordered logit. The current MIS search is defined for a linear representation, so the audit applies to the reported linear-probability robustness coefficient for the same randomized treatment contrast and does not constitute a deletion audit of the headline ordered-logit estimate.



\subsection{Interpretation of the Study-Specific Audits}
\label{app:application-interpretation-summary}

The ten audits apply a common coefficient-sensitivity question to validated paper-specific estimands. Fixed effects are represented explicitly where required, fixed weights are handled through exact transformed regressions, and model-selection steps are frozen when repeated selection would change the nuisance specification. Full-refit validation then evaluates each selected deletion set using the corresponding frozen audit specification.

The paths in \autoref{fig:application-all-paths} describe finite-sample dependence of these audited coefficients on selected observations. Early threshold crossings indicate sensitivity to small subsets, while later or absent crossings indicate greater stability over the searched deletion range. These results do not evaluate the source experiments as a whole, and they do not extend beyond the audited estimand and specification.
