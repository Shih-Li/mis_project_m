\subsection{Algorithmic and Calibration Checks}
\label{app:numerical}

The algorithmic study checks the computational components used by fixed-\(k\) MIS. Injected-set overlap and attained coefficient influence are distinct evaluation criteria: two size-\(k\) sets can differ in membership while producing nearly identical target-coefficient movement. Across the refined comparisons, both greedy and Dinkelbach-based search attained a median influence ratio of \(1.00\), while their recovered observation identities sometimes differed. The aggregate results are reported in \autoref{tab:03-algorithm-summary}.

\input{../../output/03_algorithmic/tables/main/03_tab1_algorithm_summary.tex}

The Dinkelbach formulation is retained because it globally optimises the stated fixed-residualised objective under the conditions in \autoref{sec:methodology}. Runtime comparisons, architecture-specific overlap, collinearity checks, nestedness traces, and numerical-convergence grids are retained in the repository as implementation diagnostics.

The EVT stress tests provide a separate qualification. GEV fitting can converge numerically while the resulting reference distribution remains poorly calibrated. In the renewed null experiment, rejection is strongly conservative under both fitting procedures, as shown in \autoref{fig:03-null-calibration}. These results support treating EVT as a design-specific calibration device rather than a general inferential property of fixed-\(k\) MIS.

\subsection{Supplementary Robust-Estimation Results}
\label{app:estimation-results}

The robust-estimation simulation uses MIS as an oracle-assisted localisation benchmark. The deletion budget and direction are supplied from the data-generating process, while observation identities remain unknown to the search. The oracle audit confirms that deleting the injected set produces a much larger target-coefficient shift under bad leverage than under response-outlier or good-leverage contamination (\autoref{tab:robust-oracle-audit}). This difference explains why localisation and post-deletion estimation are most closely aligned under bad leverage.

\input{../../output/04_robust/tables/supplement/04_tab_oracle_audit.tex}

The complete estimator-by-environment comparisons, coverage results, runtime diagnostics, and predictor/error-distribution breakdowns are retained in the repository. They support the same distinction developed in \autoref{subsec:estimation-performance}: influential-set localisation and robust coefficient estimation are separate statistical objectives.








\subsection{Supplementary Misspecification Results}
\label{app:misspec-definitions}

For the additive misspecification mechanisms, severity is scaled so that the standard deviation of the imposed misspecification component relative to the baseline error standard deviation equals the severity parameter \(\lambda\). Heteroskedasticity, linear endogeneity, and nonlinear confounding use mechanism-specific severity parameters. Detection boundaries are therefore interpreted within the corresponding mechanism-specific scale.

Target-coefficient sensitivity is evaluated using
\[
T_k
=
\frac{
	\left|
	\widehat{\beta}_{-S_k}-\widehat{\beta}
	\right|
}{
	\widehat{\operatorname{se}}(\widehat{\beta})
},
\]
where the selected set is obtained from the fixed-residualised search and \(\widehat{\beta}_{-S_k}\) is evaluated by full-refit validation. The denominator is the standard error from the original fitted model and is held fixed as a reference scale. \(T_k\) is not treated as a Wald statistic because the deletion set is selected adaptively and the full-sample and post-deletion estimates are dependent.

Independent null simulations provide the environment-specific reference distribution. At the primary deletion fraction, empirical null rejection remains close to the nominal \(5\%\) level across the evaluated error distributions (\autoref{tab:05-size-by-error}).

\input{../../output/05_misspecification/tables/supplement/05_tabS01_size_by_error.tex}

For an environment whose fitted power curve reaches \(0.80\), the detection boundary is the interpolated severity at which that level is first attained. Environments that do not reach the criterion within the simulated range are treated as right-censored. The complete sample-size pattern underlying \autoref{fig:05-boundary-by-n} is shown in \autoref{fig:05S-boundary-faceted}. The mechanism-specific differences reinforce the main-text conclusion that misspecification does not translate uniformly into target-coefficient sensitivity.

Detailed power curves, predictor- and error-distribution breakdowns, diagnostic-comparison grids, deletion-budget summaries, and supplementary estimation comparisons are retained in the repository. They do not alter the two distinctions emphasised in the main text: the MIS response depends on the form of misspecification, and target-coefficient localisation does not constitute model correction.
