\section{Empirical Evidence}
\label{sec:application}

This section examines the magnitude and structure of target-coefficient sensitivity across ten published studies built around randomized experimental variation. One prespecified target estimand from each study is evaluated under a common deletion protocol. The cross-study analysis measures how far each coefficient can move within a small deletion budget, while selected applications examine how influential-set membership persists, changes with \(k\), or concentrates across observable groups. Each result is conditional on the validated audit specification and realised estimation sample. Study-specific specifications, validation checks, and implementation details are reported in \autoref{app:application-details}.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Audit Design}
\label{subsec:application-design}

Each empirical audit preserves the validated specification associated with its selected estimand while applying a common deletion protocol. The estimation sample is first reproduced, and the baseline target coefficient is verified. Candidate deletion sets are then constructed in both directions of coefficient movement over deletion sizes up to approximately \(5\%\) of the estimation sample. The search uses the fixed-residualised MIS objective, after which each selected set undergoes \emph{full-refit validation} using the estimator associated with the frozen audit specification. The resulting sensitivity paths therefore record coefficient movement after re-estimation on the retained sample.

The empirical analysis uses the ten studies included in the companion replication project. Each study has author-provided replication materials, from which I construct a paper-specific R replication workflow. Entry into the cross-study MIS audit requires successful validation of the target estimation sample and baseline coefficient. The audited coefficient must also have an MIS-compatible linear representation, either through the audited specification itself or through a documented linear companion or robustness specification. One explicitly documented treatment, exposure, or policy coefficient is audited in each study. The cross-study results therefore describe sensitivity patterns within these ten audited applications and illustrate the potential value of considering estimate sensitivity more broadly in the experimental literature.

The common search requires an MIS-compatible linear representation of the target coefficient. Fixed effects, fixed weights, and nuisance specifications are retained only after baseline equivalence has been verified. When the headline estimator lies outside the current linear search framework, the audit uses a documented linear companion or robustness specification. \autoref{app:application-details} records the resulting estimand and specification for each study.

The empirical analysis targets finite-sample point-estimate sensitivity within each validated specification. Randomized assignment supplies the identifying variation in the source studies, while the deletion analysis measures dependence of the audited coefficient on its realised estimation sample. These are separate inferential objects. An experimentally identified effect can therefore exhibit substantial finite-sample coefficient sensitivity within a particular audited specification.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Magnitude of Target-Coefficient Sensitivity}
\label{subsec:application-sensitivity}

The ten audited coefficients differ substantially in how quickly they move under small deletion budgets. To compare this sensitivity across studies, the audit records the first searched deletion fraction at which the full-refit coefficient reaches a prespecified threshold relative to its full-sample value. The thresholds are a \(50\%\) attenuation, a \(50\%\) amplification, and a zero crossing. To make coefficient movement comparable across outcomes measured on different scales, \autoref{tab:application-sensitivity} also reports the original-model standard error and the standardized movement measure
\[
T_k = \frac{ \left| \widehat{\beta}_{-S_k} - \widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}} \right|
}{
	\widehat{\operatorname{se}}
	\left(
	\widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{full}}
	\right)
}.
\]
Thus, \(T_k\) expresses the full-refit coefficient movement in units of the original-model standard error. It is used here as a descriptive cross-study measure of coefficient movement rather than as a Wald test statistic. Each threshold is evaluated within a deletion budget of approximately \(5\%\) of the corresponding estimation sample. Because the search optimises the fixed-residualised objective rather than the refitted coefficient itself, each reported deletion count is an upper bound on the smallest deletion that reaches the threshold: at most that many observations suffice, and a different set of the same or smaller size may exist.

Eight of the ten audited coefficients cross zero along at least one searched deletion path within the prespecified budget (\autoref{fig:application-sensitivity-thresholds} and \autoref{tab:application-sensitivity}). The first zero crossing ranges from a single selected deletion, approximately \(0.009\%\) of the complete audit sample in the cash-versus-in-kind application, to \(63\) selected deletions, approximately \(3.46\%\), in the electricity-information application. The first searched zero-crossing fraction therefore varies markedly across the audited estimands.

The standardized movement at these crossings also differs substantially. Among the applications that cross zero, \(T_k\) at the first crossing ranges from \(0.0816\) in the cash-versus-in-kind application to \(5.86\) in the immunization application. The cash-versus-in-kind case illustrates why deletion fraction and standardized movement should be considered separately: deleting a single observation reverses the sign of the coefficient, but the baseline estimate is \(-0.886\) with a standard error of \(19.1\), so the corresponding movement is only \(0.0816\) original-standard-error units. By contrast, the immunization coefficient first crosses zero after \(214\) selected deletions, approximately \(2.90\%\) of the estimation sample, and the associated movement is \(T_k=5.86\). The deletion fraction therefore measures how concentrated the sensitivity is in the sample, whereas \(T_k\) measures the magnitude of the resulting coefficient movement relative to the baseline standard-error scale. Neither quantity is, by itself, a test of statistical significance. Additional study-specific diagnostics are reported in \autoref{app:application-details}.

The vaccine-delivery and self-esteem applications exhibit sensitivity without a sign reversal within the searched range. The vaccine-delivery estimate reaches a \(50\%\) attenuation at approximately \(4.17\%\) deletion, with \(T_k=6.45\), despite not crossing zero within the audit budget. The self-esteem coefficient reaches both the \(50\%\) attenuation and \(50\%\) amplification thresholds, with standardized movements of \(T_k=1.72\) and \(T_k=1.70\), respectively, but likewise does not cross zero. These applications show that substantial coefficient movement can occur without a sign reversal. Attenuation, amplification, and \(T_k\) therefore provide complementary descriptions of target-coefficient sensitivity.

\subsection{Structure of Influential Sets}
\label{subsec:application-interpretation}

Influential sets also differ in their composition as the deletion budget changes and across application contexts. The access-to-finance and health-information audits show contrasting patterns of persistence across values of \(k\), while the immunization audit provides evidence of geographic concentration. These patterns complement the cross-study sensitivity thresholds by characterising the structure of the observations carrying target-coefficient influence.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../../application/00_cross_study/figures/main/05_fig2_influential_set_persistence.pdf}
\caption{Influential-set persistence in two empirical applications. Panel A reports the sign-reversing search path of the access-to-finance application of \textcite{cai2024indirect} for \(k=1,\ldots,40\), where influential observations accumulate through a nested path. Panel B reports the amplifying search path of the health-information application of \textcite{ciancio2025surviving} for \(k=60,\ldots,100\), where influential-set membership changes across deletion budgets. On this path, refits are available up to \(k=94\); from \(k=95\) the refitted target coefficient is no longer finite. The dashed line marks the first zero crossing where it falls inside the displayed window.}
\label{fig:application-persistence}
\end{figure}

The access-to-finance audit combines strong coefficient sensitivity with a persistent influential core. \textcite{cai2024indirect} study direct and competitor-exposure effects of experimentally induced access to finance, and the audit targets the competitor-treatment exposure coefficient for firm log sales. Its validated full-sample estimate is \(-0.0855\), and the first sign-reversing MIS contains \(20\) of \(8{,}612\) observations, approximately \(0.23\%\) of the complete audit sample. The selected observations span \(16\) firms. Full-refit validation reproduces the sign reversal exactly; the fixed-effects estimator additionally removes two observations because the selected deletions create two singleton firm groups (see \autoref{app:application-details}). Over the range displayed in \autoref{fig:application-persistence}, the selected sets remain nested as \(k\) increases. Along the full sign-reversing path, the first non-nested transition occurs at \(k=279\).

The health-information audit shows a different structure on its other search direction. \textcite{ciancio2025surviving} study the consequences of learning an HIV-positive diagnosis, and the present audit considers their reported OLS companion specification for survival. The validated full-sample coefficient is \(-0.1566\), and it first crosses zero after deleting \(17\) of \(2{,}332\) observations, approximately \(0.73\%\) of the estimation sample, along a path that remains nested throughout the audit budget. The opposite search direction amplifies the coefficient instead, to more than five times its full-sample magnitude. Along that amplifying path, the maximising set at one budget is repeatedly not contained in the maximising set at the next, with non-nested transitions between \(k=71\) and \(k=93\) (\autoref{fig:application-persistence}). The composition of the most influential set therefore changes with the budget on this path, whereas it accumulates on the sign-reversing path. Refits on the amplifying path are available up to \(k=94\); from \(k=95\) the refitted target coefficient is no longer finite, so the path is reported only up to that point. The source study's preferred IV/GMM causal specification lies outside the current linear MIS audit.

Because each deletion budget defines a separate optimisation problem (\autoref{subsec:fixed_k_mis}), fixed-\(k\) MIS does not require the maximising sets to be nested. Along a nested search path, the selected maximising set at a larger budget extends the selected set at the preceding budget; along a non-nested path, selected maximising-set membership changes with the budget. Neither pattern is by itself evidence of robustness or of diagnostic failure: the nested access-to-finance path has a sign-reversing MIS at \(k=20\). The two applications show that influential-set structure can differ across search directions as well as across studies, so target-coefficient sensitivity has a compositional dimension in addition to the magnitude of coefficient movement.

The immunization audit provides a third structural pattern through geographic concentration. \textcite{banerjee2025selecting} evaluate randomized combinations of immunization reminders, incentives, and community ambassadors in Haryana, India. The audit targets the pooled policy coefficient for immunizations per dollar associated with the selected policy specification. Its validated full-sample estimate is \(0.0040\), and the coefficient first crosses zero after deleting \(214\) of \(7{,}370\) village-month observations, approximately \(2.90\%\) of the estimation sample.

The first sign-reversing set is unevenly distributed across the seven study districts (\autoref{fig:application-spatial-concentration}). Panipat and Mewat contribute larger shares of the selected set than of the full estimation sample, while Jhajjar and Rewari contribute smaller shares. The observations carrying coefficient influence therefore have an observable geographic structure in this application. This concentration describes the composition of the selected set without identifying the mechanism that generates its influence.

Across the ten audited coefficients, searched threshold crossings vary substantially in both deletion fraction and movement measured in original-standard-error units. The deletion fraction and \(T_k\) therefore provide complementary descriptions of sensitivity: the former measures how concentrated the relevant deletion set is in the realised sample, while the latter measures how far the refitted coefficient moves relative to the baseline standard-error scale. The selected sets also differ in persistence and observable composition, including persistent influential cores, membership turnover across deletion budgets, and geographically concentrated subsets. These patterns characterise how audited coefficients depend on particular parts of their realised estimation samples.

The empirical evidence characterises finite-sample sensitivity of the selected estimands within their validated audit specifications and stated deletion budgets. The source studies' identification strategies and other estimands remain separate objects of analysis. Study-specific specifications, validation checks, and scope qualifications are reported in \autoref{app:application-details}. Together with the simulation evidence, these audits show that target-coefficient sensitivity varies in both magnitude and structure across mechanisms and realised empirical samples.
