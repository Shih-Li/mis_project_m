\section{Empirical Applications}
\label{sec:application}

This section evaluates target-coefficient sensitivity across ten published empirical studies built around randomized experimental variation. One prespecified target estimand from each study is audited under a common deletion protocol. The audit asks how far the target coefficient can move when a small selected set is removed from its validated estimation sample. The resulting analysis characterises finite-sample coefficient sensitivity within the frozen audit specification. Study-specific specifications, validation checks, and implementation details are reported in \autoref{app:application-details}.

\subsection{Audit Design}
\label{subsec:application-design}

The empirical audit applies a common procedure while preserving the specification associated with each selected estimand. Each application first reproduces the relevant estimation sample and verifies the baseline target coefficient. Candidate deletion sets are then constructed in both directions of coefficient movement over deletion sizes up to approximately \(5\%\) of the estimation sample. Each selected set is subsequently evaluated by \emph{full-refit validation}, using the validated estimator associated with the frozen audit specification. The reported sensitivity paths therefore record coefficients obtained after re-estimating the application-specific model on the retained sample.

The common search requires an MIS-compatible linear representation of the target coefficient. Fixed effects, fixed weights, and selected nuisance specifications are represented within this framework only after baseline equivalence has been verified. Some applications consequently audit a documented linear companion or robustness specification when the headline estimator lies outside the current linear search framework. These choices define the estimand being audited. \autoref{app:application-details} documents the corresponding specification for each study and distinguishes the fixed-residualised search representation from subsequent full-refit validation.

The application focuses on point-estimate sensitivity conditional on these validated specifications. Randomized assignment provides the identifying variation in the source studies, while deletion analysis examines the finite-sample stability of the specific coefficients audited here. The two questions are distinct. An experimentally identified effect in the source study can coexist with target-coefficient sensitivity in a particular audited specification.

\subsection{Cross-Study Target-Coefficient Sensitivity}
\label{subsec:application-sensitivity}

Target-coefficient sensitivity varies substantially across the ten selected estimands. To make coefficient movements comparable across studies, the audit records the smallest deletion fraction required to produce prespecified changes from the full-sample estimate. The main thresholds are a \(50\%\) attenuation, a \(50\%\) amplification, and a crossing of zero. All thresholds are evaluated within the common deletion budget of approximately \(5\%\) of the corresponding estimation sample.

Eight of the ten selected coefficients cross zero along at least one searched deletion path within the prespecified budget (\autoref{fig:application-sensitivity-thresholds} and \autoref{tab:application-sensitivity}). The deletion fractions at which this first occurs differ sharply across studies. The cash-versus-in-kind contrast first crosses zero along the searched path after one deletion, corresponding to approximately \(0.009\%\) of its estimation sample. The access-to-finance coefficient first crosses zero after \(20\) deletions, or approximately \(0.23\%\) of its sample, while the electricity-information coefficient first crosses zero after \(63\) deletions, approximately \(3.46\%\) of its estimation sample. These differences indicate substantial heterogeneity in finite-sample stability across the selected audited coefficients.

Two audited coefficients remain on their original side of zero throughout the searched deletion budget. The vaccine-delivery estimate first reaches a \(50\%\) attenuation after approximately \(4.17\%\) of its sample is removed, while the selected self-esteem coefficient does not cross zero within the searched range. These cases show that substantial coefficient movement and sign reversal are separate sensitivity outcomes. The cross-study evidence therefore reveals considerable heterogeneity among coefficients drawn from studies based on randomized experimental variation.

\subsection{Illustrative Applications}
\label{subsec:application-interpretation}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../../application/00_cross_study/figures/main/05_fig2_influential_set_persistence.pdf}
	\caption{Influential-set persistence in two empirical applications. Panel A reports the access-to-finance application of \textcite{cai2024indirect}, where influential observations accumulate through a nested path over the displayed deletion range. Panel B reports the health-information application of \textcite{ciancio2025surviving}, where influential-set membership changes over the displayed deletion range. The dashed line marks the first zero crossing along the displayed directional path.}
	\label{fig:application-persistence}
\end{figure}

\autoref{fig:application-persistence} illustrates two forms of target-coefficient sensitivity in experimental applications. \textcite{cai2024indirect} study how experimentally induced access to finance affects treated firms and firms exposed to treated competitors. They find positive direct effects on treated firms together with negative indirect effects on firms whose competitors receive access to finance. The present audit targets the competitor-treatment exposure coefficient for firm log sales. Its validated full-sample estimate is \(-0.0855\), and the coefficient first crosses zero along the searched path after deleting \(20\) of \(8{,}612\) observations, corresponding to approximately \(0.23\%\) of the estimation sample. The audited indirect-effect coefficient is therefore sensitive to a feasible deletion set containing a small fraction of the estimation sample.

\textcite{ciancio2025surviving} examine the consequences of learning an HIV-positive diagnosis in a setting where antiretroviral treatment was initially unavailable. Among HIV-positive individuals, they find that learning one's status is associated with substantially lower subsequent survival, with their preferred causal estimates indicating a reduction of approximately \(23\) percentage points after six years. The present audit considers the reported OLS companion specification for the survival outcome, whose validated full-sample coefficient on learning HIV-positive status is \(-0.1566\). The coefficient first crosses zero along the searched path after deleting \(17\) of \(2{,}332\) observations, corresponding to approximately \(0.73\%\) of the estimation sample. The audited OLS coefficient is therefore sensitive to a feasible deletion set containing less than one percent of the estimation sample. The preferred IV/GMM causal specification studied by \textcite{ciancio2025surviving} lies outside the current linear MIS audit.

The two applications also differ in the structure of the influential sets over the displayed deletion ranges. In the access-to-finance application, observations entering the influential set at smaller deletion sizes tend to remain as the set expands. The displayed path therefore has a relatively stable core. In the health-information application, membership changes repeatedly over the displayed range, producing a non-nested path. The observations driving sensitivity can consequently depend on the permitted size of the deletion set, even when the target coefficient remains fixed.

\textcite{banerjee2025selecting} study how alternative combinations of reminders, incentives, and community ambassadors affect immunization uptake in Haryana, India. Their large-scale randomized experiment evaluates multiple policy combinations and identifies information-hub ambassadors combined with SMS reminders and no incentives as the most cost-effective policy, increasing immunizations per dollar by \(9.1\%\) relative to the status quo. The present audit targets the corresponding pooled policy coefficient for immunizations per dollar. Its validated full-sample estimate is \(0.0040\), and the coefficient first crosses zero along the searched path after deleting \(214\) of \(7{,}370\) village-month observations, corresponding to approximately \(2.90\%\) of the estimation sample.

\autoref{fig:application-spatial-concentration} shows that the sign-reversing observations are unevenly distributed across the study districts. Panipat and Mewat account for substantially larger shares of the sign-reversing MIS than of the full estimation sample, while Jhajjar and Rewari are substantially less represented. Panel A gives the corresponding geographic distribution, with \(51\) influential observations in Bhiwani, \(46\) in Panipat, and \(38\) in Mewat. The sensitivity of the pooled cost-effectiveness coefficient is therefore associated with a geographically structured subset of the realized sample.

The experimental identification strategies in the source studies and the finite-sample stability of the audited coefficients address different properties. Random assignment supplies the identifying variation in the source studies, while the MIS audit measures how strongly each audited coefficient depends on particular observations in the realized sample. These are distinct properties. In eight of the ten applications, at least one searched deletion set within the approximately \(5\%\) audit budget moves the selected coefficient across zero. \autoref{fig:application-persistence} shows that this sensitivity can arise through both stable and changing influential-set structures, while \autoref{fig:application-spatial-concentration} shows that the observations producing a sign reversal can also be unevenly distributed across geographic units.

These results apply to the audited estimands and specifications. A zero crossing along a searched deletion path establishes finite-sample sensitivity of the selected coefficient under the stated deletion budget. Causal identification, the validity of the source design, and results outside the audited specification remain separate questions. Study-specific specifications, validation results, and scope qualifications are reported in \autoref{app:application-details}.
