\section{Empirical Applications}
\label{sec:application}

This section evaluates target-coefficient sensitivity across ten published empirical studies built around randomized experimental variation. One prespecified target estimand from each study is audited under a common deletion protocol. The audit asks how far the target coefficient can move when a small selected set is removed from its validated estimation sample. The resulting analysis characterises finite-sample coefficient sensitivity within the frozen audit specification. Study-specific specifications, validation checks, and implementation details are reported in \autoref{app:application-details}.

\subsection{Audit Design}
\label{subsec:application-design}

The empirical audit applies a common procedure while preserving the specification associated with each selected estimand. Each application first reproduces the relevant estimation sample and verifies the baseline target coefficient. Candidate deletion sets are constructed in both directions of coefficient movement over deletion sizes up to approximately \(5\%\) of the estimation sample. The search uses the fixed-residualised MIS objective, after which each selected set is evaluated by \emph{full-refit validation} using the validated estimator associated with the frozen audit specification. The reported sensitivity paths therefore record coefficients obtained after re-estimating the application-specific model on the retained sample.

The common search requires an MIS-compatible linear representation of the target coefficient. Fixed effects, fixed weights, and nuisance specifications are retained only after baseline equivalence has been verified. When the headline estimator lies outside the current linear search framework, the audit uses a documented linear companion or robustness specification. \autoref{app:application-details} records the resulting estimand and specification for each study.

The application focuses on point-estimate sensitivity conditional on these validated specifications. Randomized assignment provides the identifying variation in the source studies, while deletion analysis examines the finite-sample stability of the specific coefficients audited here. The two questions are distinct. An experimentally identified effect in the source study can coexist with target-coefficient sensitivity in a particular audited specification.

\subsection{Cross-Study Target-Coefficient Sensitivity}
\label{subsec:application-sensitivity}

Target-coefficient sensitivity varies substantially across the ten selected estimands. To make coefficient movements comparable across studies, the audit records the first searched deletion fraction at which the full-refit coefficient reaches a prespecified threshold relative to its full-sample value. The reported thresholds are a \(50\%\) attenuation, a \(50\%\) amplification, and a zero crossing. All are evaluated within a deletion budget of approximately \(5\%\) of the corresponding estimation sample.

Eight of the ten audited coefficients cross zero along at least one searched deletion path within the prespecified budget (\autoref{fig:application-sensitivity-thresholds} and \autoref{tab:application-sensitivity}). The first zero crossing ranges from a single deleted observation, approximately \(0.009\%\) of the estimation sample in the cash-versus-in-kind application, to \(63\) observations, approximately \(3.46\%\), in the electricity-information application. The cross-study variation indicates that dependence on small selected subsets differs substantially across the audited coefficients.

The vaccine-delivery and self-esteem coefficients remain on their original side of zero throughout the searched range. The vaccine-delivery estimate nevertheless reaches a \(50\%\) attenuation at approximately \(4.17\%\) deletion, while the self-esteem coefficient also exhibits material movement within the audit budget. Sign reversal is therefore one sensitivity outcome among several; its absence does not imply coefficient invariance.

\subsection{Illustrative Applications}
\label{subsec:application-interpretation}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../../application/00_cross_study/figures/main/05_fig2_influential_set_persistence.pdf}
	\caption{Influential-set persistence in two empirical applications. Panel A reports the access-to-finance application of \textcite{cai2024indirect}, where influential observations accumulate through a nested path over the displayed deletion range. Panel B reports the health-information application of \textcite{ciancio2025surviving}, where influential-set membership changes over the displayed deletion range. The dashed line marks the first zero crossing along the displayed directional path.}
	\label{fig:application-persistence}
\end{figure}

\autoref{fig:application-persistence} illustrates two forms of target-coefficient sensitivity in experimental applications. \textcite{cai2024indirect} study direct and competitor-exposure effects of experimentally induced access to finance. The audit targets the competitor-treatment exposure coefficient for firm log sales. Its validated full-sample estimate is \(-0.0855\), and the coefficient first crosses zero after deleting \(20\) of \(8{,}612\) observations, approximately \(0.23\%\) of the estimation sample. This application therefore provides a case in which a small selected subset materially changes the audited coefficient.

\textcite{ciancio2025surviving} study the consequences of learning an HIV-positive diagnosis. The present audit considers their reported OLS companion specification for survival, with a validated full-sample coefficient of \(-0.1566\). The coefficient first crosses zero after deleting \(17\) of \(2{,}332\) observations, approximately \(0.73\%\) of the estimation sample. The preferred IV/GMM causal specification in the source study lies outside the current linear MIS audit.

The two applications differ in the persistence of the selected observations. In the access-to-finance audit, observations entering at smaller deletion sizes tend to remain as \(k\) increases, producing a relatively stable core. In the health-information audit, membership changes repeatedly and the path is non-nested. The observations carrying target-coefficient influence can therefore change with the deletion budget even when the audited coefficient is fixed.

\textcite{banerjee2025selecting} evaluate randomized combinations of immunization reminders, incentives, and community ambassadors in Haryana, India. The audit targets the pooled policy coefficient for immunizations per dollar associated with the selected policy specification. Its validated full-sample estimate is \(0.0040\), and the coefficient first crosses zero after deleting \(214\) of \(7{,}370\) village-month observations, approximately \(2.90\%\) of the estimation sample.

\autoref{fig:application-spatial-concentration} shows that the sign-reversing set is geographically concentrated. Panipat and Mewat contribute larger shares of the selected set than of the full estimation sample, while Jhajjar and Rewari contribute smaller shares. The coefficient sensitivity in this application is therefore associated with a geographically structured subset of the realised sample.

The empirical audits reveal substantial heterogeneity in finite-sample target-coefficient sensitivity across the ten selected estimands. Sensitivity can arise through very small sign-reversing sets, through influential sets whose membership changes with \(k\), or through subsets concentrated in particular geographic units. These patterns describe dependence of the audited coefficient on the realised estimation sample.

A zero crossing along a searched deletion path establishes finite-sample sensitivity under the stated specification and deletion budget. It does not adjudicate the source study's causal identification strategy or results outside the audited estimand and specification. Study-specific specifications, validation checks, and scope qualifications are reported in \autoref{app:application-details}.
