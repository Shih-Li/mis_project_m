\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis examines how data contamination and model misspecification generate concentrated sensitivity in a prespecified regression coefficient, and what fixed-\(k\) Most Influential Sets reveal about the structure and consequences of that sensitivity. Across the simulations and empirical audits, target-coefficient sensitivity depends on the mechanism generating the data, the fitted specification, the target coefficient, and the deletion budget. Outlyingness, joint influence, estimator stability, and the observable composition of influential sets therefore describe related but distinct properties. Fixed-\(k\) MIS provides a coefficient-targeted localisation device: for a stated deletion budget, it identifies the set that maximises coefficient movement under the fixed-residualised objective. The subsequent analyses evaluate when that localisation aligns with conventional outlyingness, how it relates to estimation, and how it appears under misspecification and in realised empirical samples.

The contamination simulation shows that concentrated target-coefficient influence depends on how the contamination mechanism interacts with the target slope. Bad leverage produces the strongest alignment between the planted contamination set and joint coefficient influence because extreme predictor positions are combined with response shifts that distort the fitted relationship. MIS consequently recovers most of the injected set in this design. Response outliers and good leverage generate weaker alignment. Large residuals can contribute limited target-slope influence, while extreme predictor positions can remain closely aligned with the fitted relation. Recovery of a planted set therefore has a mechanism-specific interpretation: it is highest when the planted observations themselves carry concentrated influence on the target coefficient.

The second simulation gives influence localisation a specific statistical interpretation. Fixed-\(k\) MIS identifies observations carrying large joint influence on the target coefficient, while MM regression and LTS seek coefficient stability under contamination. These objectives can produce different estimation consequences. Under response-outlier and good-leverage contamination, deleting the MIS-selected set can move a coefficient that was already close to its target value, whereas localisation and coefficient accuracy align more closely under bad leverage. The coefficient-error distributions also show that typical and average Monte Carlo performance can diverge when infrequent large errors affect the arithmetic mean more strongly than the median. Influence localisation and robust estimation therefore describe separate dimensions of performance.

Model misspecification provides a second source of concentrated target-coefficient sensitivity. Nonlinear confounding, heterogeneous slopes, nonlinear functional form, and threshold behaviour generate strong sensitivity responses across the recorded environments, while omitted-variable misspecification is substantially weaker in the simulated design. Linear endogeneity provides an algebraic fitted-span invariance boundary case. Its severity component lies within the fitted linear span, so increasing severity changes the coefficient representation while leaving the standardised deletion-sensitivity structure unchanged. The form of misspecification therefore determines whether coefficient sensitivity becomes concentrated under the stated deletion budget.

The structure of the selected set can also vary independently of the strength of the sensitivity signal. In the threshold design, MIS-selected observations are substantially enriched for membership in the affected subgroup, while heterogeneous slopes produce much weaker enrichment despite substantial coefficient sensitivity. The post-deletion results add a further consequence: coefficient RMSE can increase as the deletion budget grows after selected observations are removed and the model is refitted. These findings give MIS a diagnostic interpretation within a maintained specification. It localises where finite-sample coefficient sensitivity is concentrated, while the substantive meaning of that localisation depends on the mechanism generating the model departure.

The empirical audits show that target-coefficient sensitivity varies in both magnitude and structure across realised estimation samples. Eight of the ten selected coefficients cross zero along at least one searched deletion path within an approximately \(5\%\) deletion budget. The first zero crossing ranges from one observation, approximately \(0.009\%\) of the estimation sample in the cash-versus-in-kind application, to approximately \(3.46\%\) in the electricity-information application. Influential-set composition also differs across applications. Some sensitivity paths contain a persistent influential core, others change membership as \(k\) increases, and the immunization application exhibits geographic concentration. The empirical evidence therefore extends the simulation results from controlled mechanisms to heterogeneous patterns of finite-sample coefficient dependence in realised data.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Development}

Several scope conditions delimit the interpretation of these results. The deletion size \(k\) is prespecified and functions as a sensitivity budget; different values can produce different influential sets. MIS is also coefficient-specific, so the selected set can change with the target coefficient even within the same fitted model. Global optimisation in this thesis applies to the stated fixed-\(k\), fixed-residualised objective. Full-refit validation evaluates the selected candidate set after re-estimating the original specification, but it does not exhaustively optimise that refitted coefficient over all size-\(k\) deletion sets. The contamination simulations additionally use oracle-assisted deletion budgets and, in some designs, the known direction of the induced coefficient perturbation. EVT calibration remains conditional on \(k\) and becomes restrictive as the deletion-set size approaches the available block size.

The empirical audits carry additional interpretive limits. Each result concerns one selected estimand and validated specification, so coefficient sensitivity does not by itself determine the validity of the source study's identification strategy or the sensitivity of other estimands. Several applications use documented linear companion or robustness specifications because their headline estimators lie outside the current MIS search framework. The composition of an influential set also provides descriptive evidence about where coefficient dependence is concentrated, while explanation of that influence requires study-specific information. Observable features such as outcomes, covariates, geography, institutional structure, measurement, or heterogeneous effects can help characterise the selected observations without identifying a unique causal mechanism.

A first direction for future development is richer characterisation of influential-set structure within individual applications. Once MIS has located an influential set, subsequent analysis can examine baseline characteristics, treatment status, outcomes, residuals, geographic location, institutional setting, or other study-specific variables. Persistence across deletion budgets can further distinguish observations that repeatedly form an influential core from those that enter only at particular values of \(k\). Such analysis would connect coefficient sensitivity to observable empirical structure while preserving the distinction between localisation and explanation.

A second direction concerns the scope of the optimisation framework. Recent work has extended influential-set analysis from adaptive search procedures to global optimisation of fixed-\(k\) linear-regression objectives \parencite{kuschnig2021hidden,konrad2026finding}. Further work could develop coefficient-targeted subset search under structured deletion constraints and for estimands or estimators outside the current linear representation. This extension is particularly relevant to the empirical audits, where several headline specifications use instrumental-variable, GMM, or nonlinear estimators and are currently represented through documented linear companion specifications. Broader estimator coverage would allow the same coefficient-sensitivity question to be studied directly across a wider class of empirical models.

Taken together, the thesis characterises target-coefficient sensitivity as a structured property of the data mechanism, fitted specification, target coefficient, and deletion budget. Fixed-\(k\) MIS identifies the size-\(k\) set whose deletion produces the largest movement in the prespecified coefficient under the stated fixed-residualised objective. The simulations show that the resulting influence structure can align with conventional outlyingness, diverge from robust-estimation performance, or arise from model misspecification without a planted contamination set. The empirical audits show corresponding heterogeneity in both the magnitude of coefficient movement and the composition of influential sets. Influential-set analysis therefore provides a framework for locating and describing finite-sample coefficient dependence on small subsets of observations.
